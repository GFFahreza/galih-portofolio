    E-commerce: advantages and disadvantages

    e-commerce, uncountable noun

    p. 4

    Introduction

    E-commerce is the buying and selling of goods and services over the internet, whether through a company's own online store, a marketplace, or a shop on social media. In Indonesia, it has become part of everyday life: people order groceries, pay bills, and compare prices from their phones. For businesses, however, moving online brings real problems as well as clear benefits. In this essay, I look at six advantages and six disadvantages of e-commerce, and for each disadvantage I suggest how a business can reduce it.

    Advantages

    • Wider reach

      An online store is open all day, every day, and it can serve customers far beyond the seller's own city. As a result, a small business is no longer limited to the people who happen to walk past its door.

    • Lower operating costs

      Selling online removes the need for expensive shop rent and reduces the number of staff needed for each sale. For instance, a new seller can start on a marketplace or a hosted platform and move to their own website once sales grow.

    • Useful customer data

      Every click, abandoned cart, and completed checkout can be measured. This data shows a business which products and campaigns work, and it allows the business to test changes before committing to them.

    • Easy to scale

      Cloud hosting and automated order processing let a store handle seasonal spikes, such as Ramadan or year-end sales, without hiring a large temporary team. In contrast, a physical shop can only serve as many customers as its space and staff allow.

    • Personalization

      Order history and browsing behavior allow a store to recommend relevant products and send offers that match each customer's interests. Customers, in turn, find what they want more quickly.

    • Flexible payments

      Online stores can accept cards, bank transfers, e-wallets, QRIS, and pay-later services. Because customers can pay in the way they prefer, fewer of them abandon their purchase at checkout.

    Disadvantages

    • Security risks

      Online stores hold customer and payment data, so weak security can lead to data leaks, financial losses, and legal trouble under Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Law.

      How to reduce it: A business can reduce this risk by following secure development practices, keeping its software up to date, and testing its systems regularly.

    • Fraud

      Criminals may use stolen card details or hijacked accounts to place fake orders, and the seller often pays for the resulting chargebacks.

      How to reduce it: To reduce fraud, a store can add two-factor authentication, use a secure payment gateway, and watch for unusual ordering patterns.

    • Downtime

      If a website crashes during a flash sale, the business loses sales and customer goodwill at the same time.

      How to reduce it: Load testing, caching, and continuous monitoring help a site stay online when traffic reaches its peak.

    • Logistics

      Customers judge the whole experience by delivery, so a late or damaged parcel reflects badly on the seller, even when the courier is at fault. Indonesia's island geography makes this especially difficult.

      How to reduce it: Working with several courier services, offering parcel tracking, and giving honest delivery estimates can ease the problem.

    • Price pressure

      Marketplace commissions, advertising costs, and payment fees all reduce profit margins, while buyers can compare prices in seconds.

      How to reduce it: A business can protect its margins by building direct channels, such as its own website or an email list, and by offering better service and product bundles.

    • Trust and returns

      Buyers cannot touch or try a product before they pay, so some hesitate to buy and others return what they receive.

      How to reduce it: Verified reviews, accurate photos, clear size guides, and a simple return policy help build the trust that customers would otherwise gain by seeing the product in person.

    Conclusion

    In conclusion, e-commerce gives businesses of every size access to a larger market, lower costs, and better information about their customers. However, these advantages only last when the less visible parts of the business receive proper attention, especially security, delivery, and customer trust. On balance, I believe e-commerce is worth the effort for most businesses, provided that they plan for its risks from the start.
